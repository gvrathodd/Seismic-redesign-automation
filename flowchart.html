Seismic redesign of beams, columns and joints

IS 13920:2016 (with Amendments 1 & 2) · IS 456:2000 · procedure follows IITK-GSDMA EQ22, Example 5 (interior beam-column joint, Zone V) · revised after design review

Design step Code check Revision (loop back) Re-analysis loop Input / output

Goal (capacity design): beams must yield in flexure before they fail in shear (Steps 2–3), and beams must yield before columns and joints (Steps 5–7). Every check after Step 2 uses the over-strength of the bars actually provided, including slab bars, so any revision loops back and re-runs the checks after it. Forces come from structural analysis, so any change in member size sends the design back for re-analysis before it is final.

Avoiding over-design without losing safety

In seismic design extra steel is not automatically safe. Extra beam steel raises the beam's hinge moment, and everything after it (beam shear, the 1.4 column check, column shear, joint shear) is designed from that moment. So the procedure keeps every code factor and removes only simplifications:

Usual simplificationThis procedure
Beam faces designed singly reinforced, then bottom raised to ½ topMandatory bottom steel used as compression steel (doubly reinforced), least top + bottom
Capacities read from SP 16 tables at the nearest lower valueStrain compatibility for the bars actually chosen
Column capacity at Pu = 0 for the strong column checkLeast capacity over the actual combinations (Cl 7.2.1). Pu = 0 is unsafe above the balanced point.

The dashboard compares the refined design with the simplified textbook design, and with a software (STAAD / ETABS) design if one is entered. The claim that software over-designs holds only if that comparison shows it for your project.

Why each step exists

StepClauseWhat it protects against
1–2 Beam geometry & flexure6.1, 6.2Beam is the ductile fuse: strong enough for the demand, capped at 2.5% so it yields gradually.
3 Capacity-design shear6.3Flexure before shear. Links carry the shear present after both ends hinge, so shear never governs.
4 Column checks7.1Axial stress ≤ 0.40 fck keeps columns ductile; size rules give room for beam-bar anchorage.
5 Strong column – weak beam7.2.1Beams before columns. Columns 1.4× stronger than beams, so hinges form in beams, not columns.
6 Column shear7.5Columns survive the shear created when beam hinges form.
7 Joint9Joint carries beam bars at 1.25 fy; often the check that governs (EQ22 Example 5).
8 Hoops and detailing7.4–7.6, 9.2Confined concrete at hinges and joints; buildable spacing; safe laps.
Re-analysis loop–Forces stay consistent with the final member sizes.

Limits

  • One joint at a time; the far beam end and column above must be checked at their own joints.
  • No frame analysis inside the tool; it tells you when re-analysis is needed.
  • Not covered: torsion, walls, flat slabs, foundations, P-Δ beyond IS 456 slenderness moments.
